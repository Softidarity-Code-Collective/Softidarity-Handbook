# Softidarity: the five values

Five values a new person needs to know on day one. Each of them gathers several more detailed ones,
which live in the [full list](full-values.en.md) and are reviewed once or twice a year.

In Bulgarian: [values.bg.md](values.bg.md)

Last updated: 15 November 2026

---

## 1. Autonomy

**You decide what you do and how, and you protect your own limits.**

- You choose what to do and what not to do. If you do not want to, you do not do it.
- Within your own task you do not wait for permission and nobody micromanages you.
- When you are the person responsible, you decide. The decision is yours, not the loudest voice in
  the room.
- Nothing happens to a person without their agreement: we do not take on tasks in someone else's
  name and we do not announce anyone as a participant before asking.
- You do not wait, you act.
- You protect your own time and energy and you say so plainly. Someone else's no is accepted without
  explanations.

## 2. Accountability

**What we said, we do, and what we have done is ours, good or bad.**

- Every task has exactly one person responsible. Two owners means no owner.
- We do what we said by when we said, or we say in good time that it will not happen. A surprise
  costs more than a delay.
- When we are wrong, we are the first to say it. If we are the people responsible, the
  responsibility is ours, whatever happened.
- We finish the small things nobody will see: code with no forgotten TODOs, data with its source
  named, a file whose name still makes sense three months later.

## 3. Radical honesty

**We allow ourselves to be vulnerable and direct: we say the uncomfortable thing in time.**

- You challenge yourself neither to sugarcoat nor to hide: vulnerable and direct.
- We do not guess and we do not assume: we say what we need and we ask the other person what they
  need.
- Feedback is a gift, not an attack. We ask instead of accusing or shaming.
- You can say "I don't know", "I was wrong" and "I disagree" at no cost.
- We question our own opinion first and someone else's second. Anyone can be wrong, whatever their
  experience.
- Documents, decisions and money are open to everyone inside. Other people's vulnerable information
  is protected from the outside world: openness is good when it comes with consent.
- A claim travels with its source and its number. When the data contradicts the conclusion, we
  change the conclusion rather than defend it.

## 4. Impact

**We do what changes the most, in the simplest way, and we get better at it.**

- Before we start: it is fine to spend days in your imagination until you find the thing worth
  doing, rather than working on something just to be working on something. Empty busyness looks like
  work and changes nothing.
- Once we have started: the day ends with something finished, not only discussed.
- We measure ourselves by the change that reaches the outside, not by the hours spent inside.
  Elegant code that nobody uses is worth nothing.
- The 80 rule: done today at 80% usually beats done next month at 95%. We ship the useful version
  and improve it.
- The simplest solution that works. Everything added has to be maintained afterwards.
- We protect long uninterrupted blocks. A reply after two hours is not rude.
- Every yes is a no to something else, and we say it out loud.
- We judge by the result, including its second order effects.
- "I can't" means "I can't yet". Learning happens inside working hours, not after them.

## 5. Empathy

**We care for ourselves, the team and life on Earth.**

- The reason we write this software is the animals and the people who suffer. Compassion also points
  inwards: towards ourselves and towards the people on the team, not only towards those we work for.
- We take an interest in how the person is, not only in how far they have got. A burnt-out activist
  helps nobody.
- Trust in the motives, verification of the result. Checking is not distrust.
- We see people as they are and we treat them with compassion even when we disagree.
- We look for allies, not enemies. Performative identity is aesthetics, not progress.
- We do not humiliate, we do not threaten and we do not use fear as a tool.
- When someone harms another, the question is what we will repair, not who should be punished.
- We stop to look back, and the work has to be enjoyable often enough that we are still here in a
  year.
