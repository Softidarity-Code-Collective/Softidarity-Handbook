# Softidarity: the full list of values

Forty-two values in five groups. This is the reference document, not the introduction: joining the
collective starts with [the five](values.en.md), while this one is reviewed once or twice a year,
when each of us proposes at least one change or one deletion.

In Bulgarian: [full-values.bg.md](full-values.bg.md)

Last updated: 15 November 2026

---

## Freedom and responsibility

Neither exists without the other: the freedom to decide for yourself only means something if you
also carry the consequences.

- **Freedom.** We choose what to do and what not to do. Everything we do, we do because we want to;
  if we do not want to, we do not do it.
- **Consent.** Nothing happens to a person without their agreement: we do not take on tasks in their
  name, we do not announce them as a participant, and we do not share their work or their data
  before asking. A no is accepted without asking for a reason.
- **Autonomy.** Our work is ours: we decide for ourselves what to do within our task and how. We do
  not wait for permission and we do not micromanage.
- **Accountability and reliability.** What we do is ours, and we own it, good or bad. We do what we
  said by when we said, or we say in good time that it will not happen: a surprise costs more than a
  delay. When we make a mistake we tell the others; if we are the people responsible, the
  responsibility is ours, whatever happened. Freedom and autonomy come together with accountability.
- **Ownership.** Every task has exactly one person responsible: they decide and they answer for it.
  That includes handing out subtasks with their own responsible people and drawing on the expertise
  of helpers. Two owners means no owner.
- **Leadership and followship.** We do not hesitate to lead when we can, and we are humble enough to
  follow when someone else is leading well. The role moves with the task, not with a title.
- **Conscientiousness.** You leave the table clean after cooking. The small things keep the
  organisation alive: code with no forgotten TODOs, data with its source named, a file whose name
  still makes sense three months later.
- **Proactivity.** We do not wait to be given a task before noticing what is missing, and we speak
  up even when nobody asked.
- **Boundaries.** Each of us protects our own time and energy and says so plainly, instead of
  staying quiet and burning out. Someone else's boundary is accepted without explanations.
- **Deep work.** We protect long uninterrupted blocks for work that requires thinking. A reply after
  two hours is normal, not rude. It is fine not to answer on social media for hours, even days, and
  to be absent from it. In an emergency we call each other.

## Honesty and thinking

This is the group that goes first, because every value in it is uncomfortable in the short term.

- **Radical honesty.** We push ourselves to become more and more honest and not to keep the truth
  inside. Challenge yourself neither to sugarcoat nor to hide: vulnerable and direct. You talk to
  the person while it can still be fixed, not behind their back.
- **Transparency.** We keep all documents open and our decisions can be checked by anyone inside.
  That includes the money, the deadlines and the conversations with the client.
- **Privacy.** We protect other people's vulnerable information from the outside world: secrets
  entrusted to us and the personal data in our work, under GDPR. Openness is good when it comes with
  consent.
- **Tell culture.** We do not guess, we do not assume, and we do not wait for others to guess. We do
  not define anyone else's reality: we say what we need and we ask the other person what they need.
- **Feedback culture.** If you see something, say it. We ask and stay curious instead of accusing or
  shaming. When someone gives you feedback it is a gift. When you give it, check your own motives
  twice.
- **Communication.** We talk to each other. We write clearly, briefly and in time. One uncomfortable
  sentence today beats silence and assumptions a week later.
- **Intellectual humbleness.** Anyone can be wrong, whatever their experience, title or years. We
  question our own opinion first and someone else's second, and we hold our opinions as firmly as
  the evidence holds them.
- **Truth-seeking, factfulness.** A claim travels with its source and its number. When the data
  contradicts our conclusion, we change the conclusion.
- **Scout mindset.** We research, check and discuss in search of the right approach, instead of
  defending what we have already said. We think like a scout who wants to see the map, not like a
  soldier defending a position.
- **Bravery.** We take risks when they are needed, we challenge ourselves and we allow ourselves to
  be vulnerable. We say no to a client when we have to, and we raise the subject everyone is
  avoiding.

## Impact

This group is the reason we exist as a collective rather than as a hobby.

- **We work.** At the end of the day we want something finished, not only discussed. Meetings, plans
  and conversations are useful only if a product comes out of them.
- **Effectiveness.** We measure ourselves by the change that reaches the outside, not by the hours
  spent inside. Elegant code that nobody uses is worth nothing.
- **Speed.** We ship the useful version and improve it instead of waiting for the ideal one. We do
  not let perfect be the enemy of good: done today at 80% usually beats done next month at 95%.
- **Minimalism and simplicity.** The best solution is the simplest one that works: fewer features,
  fewer dependencies, fewer layers, shorter texts. Everything added has to be maintained afterwards.
- **Prioritization.** Time is short, so every yes is a no to something else, and we say it out loud.
  A list with no order is not prioritised. It is also fine to spend days in your imagination until
  you find the thing worth doing, rather than working on something just to be working on something.
- **Consequentialism.** We judge actions by their results, including their second and third order
  effects. But the end does not justify the means: if something requires deceiving someone, we do
  not do it.
- **Impartiality.** We help where the need is greatest, not where it is closest or most pleasant for
  us. This applies to choosing projects and to deciding who is right in an argument.
- **Readiness for success.** We also prepare for the version where it works: what we do if the
  project takes off, if more clients arrive, if we have to become more people. Unprepared success
  breaks small organisations more often than failure does.

## People

Without this group the three above turn into a collective nobody wants to stay in for a second year.

- **Compassion.** The reason we write this software is the animals and the people who suffer.
  Compassion also points inwards: towards ourselves and the rest of the team, not only towards those
  we work for.
- **Care.** We take an interest in how the other person is, not only in how far they have got, and
  each of us looks after ourselves too. A burnt-out activist helps nobody, least of all the animals.
- **Psychological safety.** You can say "I don't know", "I was wrong" or "I disagree" at no cost. A
  mistake is information, not an offence.
- **Trust.** We assume the other person is acting in good faith, and we still review the work: trust
  in the motives, verification of the result. Checking is not distrust, it is part of the craft;
  trust is the default and it is lost through actions, not through doubts.
- **Relational literacy.** Relationships are a skill that is learned, not a given: we learn to
  recognise and name what we need, to listen, and to stay connected through conflict. The aim is
  relationships that hold under strain, because a collective breaks along the human line, not the
  technical one.
- **Respect.** We see people as they are, in full, and we treat them with compassion even when we
  disagree.
- **We look for allies, not enemies.** Performative identity is a trap: "we are punks, so we hate
  hippies" and "we are anarchists, so we wind up patriots" are aesthetics rather than politics, and
  they win nobody except the approval of our own camp. Our values differ from those of most people,
  and the mature way is to let the other person understand our point of view, without judgement and
  without violence.
- **Non-violence.** Non-violence applies to language as much as to methods, inside the collective as
  much as in campaigns. We do not humiliate, we do not threaten, and we do not use fear as a tool.
- **Transformative justice and repair.** When someone harms another, the question is what we will
  repair and how, not who should be punished or excluded. The aim is a restored relationship and
  changed conditions, not winning the conflict.
- **Fun.** The work has to be enjoyable often enough that we still want to be here in a year. A
  joke, food and excessive enthusiasm about some detail are not wasted time.

## Learning

A group of its own, because this is the only part that does not show up in the result immediately,
and so it is the first thing cut when we are in a hurry.

- **Growth mindset.** Nobody knows how to fix the world, so we assume every skill can be learned,
  and "I can't" means "I can't yet". The failure of one project is the basis for the success of the
  next.
- **Adventure mindset.** We say yes to things we have not done before and treat them as an adventure
  rather than a threat. The unknown is part of the pleasure, not a risk to be avoided.
- **Reflection.** We stop and look back: what worked, what did not, and why. We treat everything as
  a stage along the way rather than a verdict.
- **Self-development.** We read, we take courses, we listen to podcasts and we set ourselves tasks
  we cannot do yet. Learning happens inside working hours, not after them.
